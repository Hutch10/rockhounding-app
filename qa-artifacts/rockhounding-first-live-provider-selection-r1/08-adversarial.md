# Adversarial notes

Retrieval date: 2026-09-23.

## SGMC

A. Zero polygons mean the query returned no map units. They do not mean no geology.
B. Surficial units, Alaska, Hawaii, and seams at state lines can be absent from this compilation while rock is present.
C. A page that hits 2000 records is partial until another page is retained. An interrupted page stays partial.
D. Geologic age text is not source-update time. Retrieval time is not publication time.
E. An unknown lithology value or an unexpected layer schema quarantines.
F. The polygon is a public map unit. It is not an exact private locality. Disclosure still runs.
G. No secret.
H. Public-domain USGS content supports a reviewed `AUTOMATED_QUERY` grant with attribution. It does not grant bulk redistribution.
I. Public display of the map unit is a separate disclosure decision. This selection does not grant `PUBLIC_API`.
J. An outage is a failed fetch. It does not make the ground accessible, collectable, or geologically empty.
K. Replay uses the saved raw response, DOI, and adapter version. A later GeMS file does not rewrite that fixture.
L. Lithology and age do not become collection permission, claim status, or safety.

## NWS

A. Zero active alerts mean none matched the filter.
B. A zone query can omit county-based warnings that exist.
C. A truncated alert page is not a complete hazard picture.
D. CAP and API times are not the platform retrieval time.
E. Unknown event codes can quarantine.
F. Alert geometry is public. The risk is meaning, not a hidden fossil site.
G. User-Agent only, today.
H. NWS says any purpose. This platform would still grant only the one operation.
I. Redisplay is separate.
J. An outage must not become "safe."
K. A saved CAP document can be replayed. The live feed will change.
L. An alert is not geology, access, or collection law.

## FIRMS

A. Zero detections are not proof of no fire.
B. Cloud, swath gaps, and sensor choice can hide fire.
C. A quota or partial day range is partial coverage.
D. Acquisition time is not retrieval time.
E. A new sensor code quarantines until the adapter names it.
F. Pixels are public. They are not private sites.
G. A map key is required.
H. Open use with citation. Training and redistribution stay separate here.
I. Sharing imagery has its own citation rule.
J. A missed detection must not become "no fire."
K. A saved detection file can be replayed. The live product moves.
L. A hotspot is not a closure, a permit, or a fire boundary.

## USMIN

A. Zero deposits do not mean no minerals.
B. The database is the most important deposits, not every occurrence.
C. A partial WFS page is partial.
D. Deposit vintage and retrieval time are different, and many ages may be unknown.
E. Unknown commodity or status codes quarantine.
F. Exact mine points are a disclosure and collecting-inference risk.
G. No key on the documented WFS.
H. USGS public-domain terms can support a query. They do not make a deposit a collecting site.
I. Public display of exact points is a separate disclosure choice.
J. An outage must not become "no deposits."
K. A saved feature can be replayed.
L. A deposit is not a claim and not collection permission.

## MLRS

A. Zero mapped claims do not mean claim-free.
B. Yes. The service says some cases cannot be geocoded.
C. A 2000-record page is not the full claim set.
D. Case disposition and retrieval time are different.
E. Unknown disposition or product codes quarantine.
F. Claim polygons are public records, not specimen points. They are still a sensitive land-status display.
G. No key on the description page.
H. A public map service can support a query. It does not adjudicate the claim.
I. Showing claim polygons publicly is a separate decision.
J. An outage or a hole in the map must not become "open to claiming" or "open to collecting."
K. A saved extract can be replayed. The case system will change.
L. A claim polygon is not geology, access, or collection permission.
